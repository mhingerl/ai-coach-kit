# Exercise & workout library

**Purpose:** a reference catalogue of strength exercises and endurance workouts (cycling, running, swimming and rowing). Every part is optional: keep the parts for the athlete's sports and delete the rest. The coach uses it to:
- compose strength sessions on the day, including superset pairings
- plan interval progressions
- suggest new exercises or formats at the right moment, for example when equipment changes or a new training phase starts

It's a menu, not a plan. The annual training plan and the strength log decide what actually gets done.

**Tailoring (done by the coach during setup, then kept up to date):**
- Fill in the "My kit?" column for the athlete's actual equipment, and write that equipment into the line below.
- Mark exercises the athlete already does with ★.
- Delete the parts for sports the athlete doesn't do, if they want a leaner document.
- For any sport the athlete does that isn't covered here (e.g. swimming, rowing, XC skiing), add a part in the same format: zones, progression rules, workout tables with progression ladders, testing, and key sources.
- Add dated notes ("added <date>") when something new is tried or learned.

**Athlete's equipment:** _(filled in during setup)_

# Part 1 — Strength exercises

## How to read the tables

- **Equipment:**
  - BW = bodyweight
  - KB = kettlebell
  - DB = dumbbell
  - BB = barbell
  - B = resistance band
  - C/M = cable or machine
  - Box = sturdy box or bench
  - Bar = pull-up bar
  - Slider = towel on a smooth floor, or sliders
- **Min kit?** Whether it works with a minimal home setup (bodyweight, bands, a pair of light kettlebells or dumbbells, a pull-up bar):
  - ✓ = yes
  - (✓) = yes, but load is limited or it needs a sturdy anchor or surface
  - ✗ = needs a gym or specific equipment
- **My kit?** Same symbols, for the athlete's actual equipment. Filled in during setup.
- **Barely loads:** muscle groups the exercise leaves mostly fresh. Pair it in a superset with an exercise from those groups.
- **Why:**
  - CY = cycling performance
  - SP = sprints, surges and race power
  - OF = counters desk posture and hip flexors shortened from sitting
  - PT = injury prevention or common physio use
  - RU = running
- **★** = already part of the athlete's repertoire (marked during setup).

**Load guidance by goal:**

| Goal | Load / reps | Proximity to failure |
|---|---|---|
| Maximal strength | 3–6 reps | 1–3 reps short of failure |
| Hypertrophy / general strength | 6–15 reps | 0–3 reps short |
| Muscular endurance | 15+ reps | Endurance training already covers this, so it's rarely worth gym time |
| Power | 3–6 fast reps, light to moderate load | Stop well short of failure; speed is the point |

For endurance athletes, heavy strength work (roughly 4–10 reps) is what improves economy and sprint power. High-rep work adds little on top of riding or running.

**Progression when load is capped (e.g. light home weights):** once all rounds are clean at the top of the rep range, make each rep harder instead of adding reps: a slower lowering (3–4 s), a 1–2 s pause at the bottom, or 1.5 reps, so failure comes at about 10–12 reps.

**Why power work:** evidence supports heavy strength work (the Rønnestad studies) plus explosive work for endurance athletes who race with sprints and surges. It improves peak power and the ability to hold power late in a race without reducing endurance, as long as endurance training continues. Plyometrics are low volume but hard on the nervous system. Do them fresh, early in a session, and never before key interval days.

## Superset pairing cheat sheet

| First exercise (heavy/priority) | Good partner |
|---|---|
| Single-leg RDL / hip hinge | Pull-up, push-up, overhead press, Pallof press |
| Bulgarian split squat / lunge / squat | Row, face pull, dead bug, hanging knee raise |
| Glute bridge / hip thrust | Row, push-up, overhead press |
| Calf raise | Clam shells, Copenhagen plank, face pull, core exercise |
| Nordic curl | Push-up, Pallof press (keep the curl fresh; it's eccentric-heavy) |
| Plyometrics | Mobility or scapular work only (keep the jumps fresh) |
| Pull-up | Leg exercise or anti-extension core |

Avoid pairing two exercises that both need hip or core stability under heavy load. For example, RDL with a heavy carry tires the lower back twice.

## Ideas for expanding a repertoire

Proposals, not decisions. The coach raises them at the right moment (a phase change, new equipment, a plateau).

**With minimal kit:**
- Seated / bent-knee calf raise for the soleus, often missing from calf work.
- Nordic curl anchored under a sofa: the best-evidenced hamstring-injury prevention, and useful for sprinting and running. Start with 2–3 × 3–5 slow lowerings.
- Chin tucks and Y-T-W raises for neck and posture on long rides and at the desk.
- Suitcase or farmer carries as a cheap core finisher.
- Hip hike as a glute-medius variant.
- Tibialis raise and pogo hops for runners.

**With gym access:**
- Heavy main lifts for a strength block: trap-bar deadlift or hip thrust (posterior chain), front squat or leg press (quads), chest-supported row and lat pulldown (pull).
- Heavy single-leg work: BSS and step-ups with real dumbbells.
- Heavy seated calf raise on a machine.

**Build and race-prep phases:**
- Swap part of the strength volume for power work: squat jumps, box jumps, KB swings with a heavier bell, med ball throws, sled pushes.
- Keep it at 1–2 short power blocks a week, placed away from key interval sessions.

# Part 2 — Cycling workouts

## Zones used here (% of FTP, Coggan-style)

| Zone | % FTP | Name | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | < 55% | Recovery | Very easy |
| Z2 | 56–75% | Endurance | Conversational |
| Z3 | 76–87% | Tempo | Focused, can still talk in short sentences |
| SS | 88–94% | Sweet spot | Hard but sustainable |
| Z4 | 95–105% | Threshold | Hard; 20–60 min maximum |
| Z5 | 106–120% | VO2max | Very hard; 3–8 min efforts |
| Z6 | 121–150% | Anaerobic capacity | 30 s – 2 min efforts |
| Z7 | > 150% / max | Neuromuscular | Sprints under 15 s |

**No power meter?** Use the same zones with these stand-ins, and take the athlete's own threshold HR (LTHR) from intervals.icu: Z1 below ~80% of LTHR, Z2 ~80–89%, Z3 ~90–94%, Z4/sweet spot ~95–100%, Z5 above ~100% (HR lags: for efforts under ~4 min go by RPE and the first minutes' effort, not HR). RPE: Z2 3–4 out of 10, tempo 5, sweet spot/threshold 6–7, VO2max 8–9. Write steps as % of threshold HR or an HR range plus an RPE cue, and judge progress by HR at the same effort, drift and completed reps instead of watts. Percent bands are rough; the athlete's own tests and platform zones win.

All targets here are **% of FTP on purpose**, never fixed watts, because FTP changes. When scheduling or recommending a session, convert to watts using the *current* FTP from intervals.icu (sport settings, or eFTP if it's clearly more recent). Recheck it at each phase transition and after every FTP test.

## General progression rules for intervals

**Order of progression.** Change one variable at a time, in this order:
1. **More repetitions** at the same duration and power (e.g. 3×4 → 4×4 → 5×4).
2. **Longer repetitions** once you reach the rep cap, usually dropping back one rep (e.g. 5×4 → 4×5 → 4×6 → 4×8).
3. **Less rest** once duration is at its cap, for sweet spot and threshold work (e.g. 5 min rest → 3 min → 2 min).
4. **Higher power** only once duration and reps are at the top of their range and the session feels controlled. Raise targets by retesting or updating FTP rather than creeping up the %. A creeping % hides the fact that FTP has changed.

**When to progress:**
- all reps completed at target power
- power held across the last rep (no fade of more than ~3%)
- last rep's effort no higher than planned (about 8 out of 10 for VO2max, 7–8 for threshold)
- this has happened in **2 sessions in a row**

**When to hold or step back:**
- A missed rep, a fading last rep, or HR spiking early means repeat the same session next time. Don't progress.
- Two failed sessions in a row means going back one step, and checking recovery (sleep, HRV, overall load).

**Track time in zone, not just the workout name.** Rough weekly targets during intensive phases:
- VO2max: about 12–25 min at Z5 per session, 1–2 sessions a week
- Threshold: about 30–60 min at Z4/SS per session, 1–2 sessions a week

**Deloads and retests:**
- Every 3–4 weeks, take a lighter week: about 30–40% less volume, and intensity cut down to a single short session.
- Retest FTP (or at least update eFTP) every 6–8 weeks.

## Tempo and sweet spot (Z3 / SS)

A time-efficient builder in base phases. Easy to overuse; it's the gray zone in a polarized week, so keep it to about 1–2 sessions a week in base phases.

**Sweet spot ladder** (88–94% FTP; move up a step when the current one is controlled):

3 × 10 min (5 min rest) → 3 × 12 → 3 × 15 → 2 × 20 → 3 × 20 → 2 × 30 → 1 × 45 → 1 × 60 → 2 × 45 (long base weeks only)

**Tempo:** 2 × 30 → 2 × 45 → 1 × 90 min at 76–87% FTP. Good inside long rides ("tempo blocks on climbs").

## VO2max (Z5)

The aim is **time at or above ~90% of VO2max**, not just time at the target power. HR needs 1–2 minutes to rise, so very short intervals only work with short recoveries.

| Workout | Structure | Evidence / notes | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 × 4 min | 4 min at 106–120% / 3–4 min easy | Helgerud 2007: the classic proven protocol | 3×3 → 4×3 → 5×3 → 4×4 → 5×4 → 6×4 → 5×5 → 4×6 |
| 5 × 5 / 4 × 6 min | At 106–112% | Longer reps, slightly lower power | Extends from 4×4 |
| 4 × 8 min | At ~105–108% / 2 min rest | Seiler 2013: bigger gains than 4×4 or 4×16 in recreational cyclists | 3×8 → 4×8. Raise power slightly once controlled. |
| **30/15 short intervals (Rønnestad)** | 3 sets of 13 × (30 s at ~120–130% / 15 s at 50%), 3 min between sets | Rønnestad 2015/2020: better improvements than 4×5 min in trained cyclists | 2 × 10 → 3 × 10 → 3 × 13, then raise the 30 s power or add a 4th set |
| 40/20 | 3–4 sets of 8–10 × (40 s at 120% / 20 s easy) | Similar principle to 30/15 | Add reps per set, then sets |
| 30/30 (Billat) | 2–3 sets of 10–12 × (30 s at 120–130% / 30 s at 50–60%) | Easier entry into short intervals | Shorten rest to 15 s → leads into 30/15 |
| Hard-start VO2max | 4–5 × 5 min: first 1 min at ~130%, then 4 min at 105–110% | Rønnestad's group and others (~2020–2022): faster VO2 rise, more time near VO2max | Advanced; for when 5×4 is easy |
| Tabata-style | 8 × 20 s all-out / 10 s rest | Very hard, anaerobic-heavy | Occasional variation; not a core session |

**Using VO2max sessions:**
- In build phases, alternate the long-rep format (4×4 / 4×8) with the short-interval format (30/15) every 1–2 weeks.
- Schedule them fresh: never the day after heavy leg strength, ideally after a rest or easy day.

## Anaerobic capacity (Z6) and neuromuscular (Z7)

| Workout | Structure | Purpose | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-min repeats | 6–8 × 1 min at 130–150% / 3–4 min rest | Anaerobic capacity (W′), handling attacks | 4 → 6 → 8 reps; then shorten the rest |
| 30 s sprints (sprint interval training) | 4–6 × 30 s all-out / 4 min rest | Anaerobic power; VO2max gains for a small time cost | Add reps. Keep each one truly all-out. |
| Short sprints | 6–10 × 8–12 s max / 3–5 min full rest | Peak power | Vary starts: seated, standing, rolling slow, rolling fast, different gears |
| Standing / big-gear starts | 6 × 10 s from near standstill in a big gear | Torque; relaunching out of corners | Low reps; full recovery |
| Micro-bursts | 2–3 sets of 10 min of 15 s at 150% / 15 s at 50% | Repeatability under fatigue | Longer sets, or 20/20 |

## Race-specific and advanced sessions

| Workout | Structure | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Crit simulation | 3–4 × 10–12 min at tempo (80–85%) with a 10–15 s surge at 150–200% every 60–90 s, final 30 s sprint | Repeated relaunches with incomplete recovery |
| Surge-and-settle | 4–6 × (2 min at 120% → 6 min at 95%) | Attacking, then holding a breakaway pace |
| Sprint from fatigue | After 60–90 min including a threshold block: 3–5 × 15 s sprints | Finishing sprint on tired legs |
| **Durability (late-ride intervals)** | 3–4 h Z2, then in the last hour 3 × 10 min SS or 4 × 4 VO2max | Holding power after 2,000+ kJ of work; the main separator in long races and group rides |
| Group ride as a structured session | Real surges from the group, plus a 0% skip-buffer block in the planned workout | Race practice with real pack dynamics |
| Low-cadence torque intervals | 4–6 × 5 min at SS power, 50–60 rpm | Muscular endurance. **Evidence is mixed**; treat it as variety. |

## Testing

| Test | Protocol | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ramp test | +20–25 W/min until failure; FTP ≈ 75% of best 1-min power | Easy to repeat; tends to overestimate FTP for riders strong in short efforts |
| 20-min test | After a proper warm-up and openers: 20 min all-out; FTP ≈ 95% | Classic; needs good pacing |
| Critical power test | 3-min and 12-min all-out on separate days (or 30–40 min apart) | Gives CP (≈ FTP) and W′ |
| eFTP (intervals.icu) | Estimated automatically from hard efforts | Fine between tests, as long as hard efforts happen regularly |

# Part 3 — Running workouts

## Zones (% of threshold pace or threshold HR)

Threshold pace ≈ the pace you could race for about 50–60 min (roughly 10 km pace for fast runners, half-marathon pace for slower ones). Threshold HR (LTHR) comes from a 30-min test (see Testing).

| Zone | % of threshold pace | % LTHR | Name | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | slower than ~78% | < 85% | Recovery | Very easy |
| Z2 | ~78–88% | 85–89% | Easy / aerobic | Conversational; most running happens here |
| Z3 | ~88–95% | 90–94% | Steady / marathon-ish | Comfortably hard; gray zone |
| Z4 | ~95–102% | 95–100% | Threshold | "Comfortably hard"; 20–40 min total per session |
| Z5 | ~103–110% | > 100% | VO2max (~3–5 km pace) | Hard; 2–5 min reps |
| Z6 | faster | — | Speed / strides | Fast but relaxed, never a sprint to failure |

As with cycling, store targets as % of threshold and convert to actual pace from the current threshold in intervals.icu. In heat, on hills or on trails, go by HR or effort instead of pace.

## Running-specific rules

- **Tissue load is the limiter, not fitness.** Bones, tendons and joints adapt more slowly than the heart and lungs, so a fit cyclist can run faster than their legs can tolerate.
- **Volume:** increase weekly running time by about 10% at most, and hold every 3rd or 4th week. After a break or injury, restart at about half the previous volume.
- **Change one thing at a time:** volume, intensity, surface, or shoes. Not two in the same week.
- **Mostly easy:** roughly 80% of running time in Z1–Z2.
- **Long run:** no more than about 30% of weekly running volume, and add at most 10–15 min per step.
- **New runners:** run/walk intervals (e.g. 1 min run / 1 min walk → 5/1 → continuous) are a legitimate way to build up.
- **Pain rule:** mild discomfort (up to about 3/10) that settles within 24 h is acceptable. Pain that changes your gait, gets worse during the run, or is still there the next morning means stop and back off; persistent pain needs a physio.
- **Strength that matters most for runners:** calf and soleus work, single-leg strength (split squats, single-leg RDL), hip abductors, and light plyometrics (pogo hops, bounds). See Part 1, rows marked RU.
- **Combined with cycling:** count running load in the weekly total, and don't put a hard run right before a key bike session or long ride.

## Easy and long runs

| Workout | Structure | Purpose | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recovery run | 20–40 min Z1 | Recovery, low extra stress | If in doubt, rest or cross-train instead |
| Easy run | 30–75 min Z2 | Aerobic base, tissue tolerance | Add ~5–10 min per week within the 10% rule |
| Easy run + strides | Easy run, then 4–8 × 15–20 s fast and relaxed, full walk-back recovery | Running economy and leg speed at almost no cost | 4 → 6 → 8 strides; do them 1–2× a week all year |
| Long run | 60–150 min Z2 | Endurance, durability | +10–15 min per step; fuel at 30–60 g/h beyond ~90 min |
| Long run with fast finish | Long run with the last 10–20 min at Z3 | Durability, race-specific fatigue | Only once the plain long run is comfortable |

## Threshold (Z4)

| Workout | Structure | Purpose | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cruise intervals | 4–6 × 5 min at threshold / 1 min jog | Threshold with low injury risk | 4×5 → 5×5 → 6×5 → 4×8 → 3×10 |
| Tempo run | 15–30 min continuous at threshold | Sustained threshold | 15 → 20 → 25 → 30 min |
| Long threshold intervals | 3 × 10–12 min at threshold / 2 min jog | Builds on cruise intervals | 3×10 → 3×12 → 2×15 |
| Norwegian-style double threshold | AM and PM sessions of short threshold reps (e.g. 5 × 6 min, 8–10 × 1 km) at controlled effort | Lots of threshold volume | Only for experienced, high-volume runners |

## VO2max (Z5)

| Workout | Structure | Purpose | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 × 4 min | 4 min at ~3–5 km pace / 3 min jog | Classic VO2max (Helgerud 2007) | 3×3 → 4×3 → 4×4 → 5×4 |
| 1 km repeats | 4–6 × 1 km at ~5 km pace / 2–3 min jog | VO2max, pacing practice | 4 → 5 → 6 reps, then shorten rest |
| 30/30 | 2 sets of 8–12 × (30 s fast / 30 s jog) | Gentler entry into VO2max work | Add reps, then a 3rd set |
| Hill repeats | 6–10 × 60–90 s uphill hard, jog down | VO2max and strength with less impact than flat reps | Add reps, then lengthen the hill |

## Speed and strength

| Workout | Structure | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short hill sprints | 6–10 × 8–10 s steep uphill, max effort, 2–3 min walk back | Power and tendon stiffness; low injury risk | Great first "speed" session for new runners |
| 200 m repeats | 6–10 × 200 m at ~mile pace / 200 m jog | Speed and economy | Keep form relaxed |
| Drills | A-skips, B-skips, high knees, butt kicks, 2 × 20 m each | Coordination | Before quality sessions |

## Testing

| Test | Protocol | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 30-min time trial | After warm-up, run 30 min as hard as you can sustain. Average HR over the last 20 min ≈ LTHR; average pace ≈ threshold pace | The standard for setting zones |
| 5 km time trial or parkrun | All-out 5 km | Estimate threshold pace from a race calculator |
| intervals.icu estimates | Threshold pace and LTHR from hard efforts | Fine between tests if hard runs happen regularly |

# Part 4 — Swimming workouts

Evidence base: swim training is less standardised than cycling or running, and these are well-established practical formats rather than exact study protocols. Technique and frequency matter more than raw volume.

## Zones (from critical swim speed, CSS)

CSS pace per 100 m comes from a 400 m and a 200 m time trial (rested, same pool): speed = 200 m ÷ (T400 − T200) in m/s; pace per 100 m = 100 ÷ speed. Retest every 6–8 weeks.

| Zone | Pace vs CSS (per 100 m) | Name | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | CSS + 12 s or slower | Recovery / drills | Very easy, focus on form |
| Z2 | CSS + 6–12 s | Aerobic | Steady, can hold a conversation at the wall |
| Z3 | CSS + 2–5 s | Tempo | Comfortably hard |
| Z4 | CSS ± 2 s | Threshold | Hard but controlled, short rest |
| Z5 | CSS − 3 s or faster | VO2max / speed | Very hard, long rest |

HR is slow and unreliable in water, so go by pace per 100 m, stroke count and RPE. Equipment (pull buoy, paddles, fins) changes the pace: note it and don't compare across equipment.

## Workouts

| Workout | Structure | Purpose | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technique | 400–800 m of drills (catch-up, fingertip drag, single-arm) with easy swims between | Stroke efficiency | Add one drill; keep Z1 |
| Aerobic base | 6–10 × 200 m Z2, 20 s rest | Aerobic endurance | More repeats, then 300 m |
| Long continuous | 1,500–3,000 m Z2 | Endurance, race-specific for open water | +200–300 m per step |
| CSS repeats | 8–12 × 100 m at CSS, 10–15 s rest | Threshold | More repeats → 5 × 200 m → 3 × 400 m at CSS |
| Threshold | 4 × 400 m at CSS + 3 s, 20 s rest | Sustained speed | Shorter rest, then pace |
| Speed | 8–16 × 25–50 m fast, 30–45 s rest | Neuromuscular speed, turns | More repeats, full rest |
| Race-pace | Race-distance share at goal pace in sets (e.g. 3 × 500 m) | Pacing | Longer sets, shorter rest |

Rules: swim two to four times a week if possible (frequency beats duration); keep most volume easy; change one variable at a time (repeats, then distance, then rest, then pace); progress after two good sessions in a row.

# Part 5 — Rowing workouts

Evidence base: indoor rowing and sculling have a well-developed training tradition (mostly low-intensity volume plus a little high intensity), but protocols vary by coach. Treat the numbers as starting points.

## Zones (from a 2 km test or a 30-min maximal piece)

| Zone | Effort | Stroke rate | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| UT2 | about 55–70% of 2 km power, conversational | 18–20 spm | Aerobic base |
| UT1 | about 70–80% | 20–24 spm | Hard aerobic |
| AT | about 80–90% (30-min race pace) | 24–28 spm | Threshold |
| TR | about 90–100% (2 km pace) | 28–32 spm | Transport / VO2max |
| AN | above 2 km pace | 32+ spm | Anaerobic / sprints |

Use the split per 500 m (or power on an ergometer), stroke rate and RPE; HR is a cross-check. Use the platform's rowing threshold if it has one.

## Workouts

| Workout | Structure | Purpose | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady state | 30–90 min UT2 | Aerobic base | +5–10 min per week; one long piece a week |
| Pyramid | 250 / 500 / 750 / 1,000 / 750 / 500 / 250 m at UT1 | Hard aerobic, pacing | Shorten the rest, then raise the pace |
| 4 × 4 min | 4 min at TR, 3 min easy | VO2max | 3 → 4 → 5 reps, then pace |
| 8 × 500 m | At 2 km pace, 90 s rest | Race-specific speed | Shorter rest, then pace |
| Threshold | 3 × 10 min at AT, 3 min easy | Sustained power | 2 × 20 min → 30 min continuous |
| Test | 2 km all-out, or 30 min maximal | Sets zones | Every 6–8 weeks, rested, same setup |

Rules: mostly UT2 (about 80% of time); keep form under fatigue and stop the piece when technique goes; low-back and rib care matter, so build volume gradually, and pair rowing with core and hip-hinge work from Part 1.

# Periodization quick reference (ties to the annual plan)

| Phase | Endurance | Intensity focus | Strength focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Z2 volume, long sessions | Sweet spot/tempo or cruise intervals; occasional VO2max to keep the ceiling up; strides for runners | Hypertrophy to strength: 6–12 reps, moving toward 4–8 reps |
| Build 1 | Keep one long session | Threshold ladder plus 1–2 VO2max sessions/week (alternating formats) | Heavy 3–6 reps with lower volume; start power work |
| Build 2 / race-specific | Endurance maintained | Race-specific work (e.g. crit simulation, over-unders, race-pace runs); VO2max maintained | Maintenance (1–2 heavy sets/exercise) plus power |
| Race season | As needed | Racing replaces some intervals | Maintenance 1–2×/week |
| Deload weeks | −30–40% volume | One short intensity touch | One light session |
